# Chapter 1

"Jaiden. *Jaiden.*"

The voice came from very far away, and Jaiden decided, with the full authority of a fifteen-year-old who had been dreaming about something warm, that he could safely ignore it.

"Jaiden Marlock, if you are not out of that bed in the next five seconds I will come up there and I will *not* be gentle."

Sofia. He knew that particular register of her voice the way he knew the creak of the third stair. It was the register that meant she had already said his name twice and was now deciding whether to escalate. He buried his face in the pillow and did the math in his head. Five seconds. He could get another five seconds out of this bed if he pretended to be asleep.

The bedroom door opened.

"Four."

He sat up so fast his head spun.

"I'm awake," he said. "I'm awake, I'm awake—"

Sofia stood in the doorway with her arms crossed, and even half-asleep, bleary and blinking against the light, Jaiden registered that she was not in her usual morning mood. Her grey eyes were doing that thing they did when she was worried and pretending not to be, and her mouth was set in a line that meant there was no arguing with what came next.

"Do you know what day it is?" she asked.

Jaiden knew. He'd known for weeks. Every fifteen-year-old in the world knew, because it was the one day nobody got to skip, no matter who their parents were, no matter how far from anywhere they lived. But he also knew Sofia well enough to know that she wanted to say it, so he let her.

"Your awakening," she said, and the word landed in the quiet house like a stone in still water. "You're to be at the Academy altar before the ninth bell, and you are still in bed."

---

The house was small, and it had never felt smaller than it did that morning.

Jaiden ate the breakfast Sofia put in front of him without tasting it — bread, and an egg, and a cup of water that he drank too fast. Across the table, Sofia was not eating at all. She was watching him, and there was something in the way she watched him that morning that made the food sit wrong in his stomach.

He was used to the way Sofia watched him. She had watched him his whole life, the way other people's parents watched their children at the edge of deep water — not hovering, exactly, but never quite looking away. He'd asked her once, when he was small, why she always looked at him like that.

*Because you're the only thing in this world that's mine,* she'd said. *And I intend to keep you in one piece.*

He'd laughed then. He didn't laugh now.

"Sofia," he said, and stopped.

She looked up.

"Nothing," he said. "I'm nervous."

It wasn't a lie, exactly. It just wasn't the whole truth. The whole truth was that he'd been nervous for weeks, and it had nothing to do with the altar. The whole truth was that sometimes, when he was alone, when the house was quiet and his mind went still, he felt something *moving* under his skin — something that had no name and no reason to be there, something that felt less like a heartbeat and more like two heartbeats, layered on top of each other, faintly, wrong.

He had never told Sofia. He didn't know how to put it into words. And some part of him, the part that had been raised to keep secrets the way other children kept toys, understood without being told that this was a thing you did not say out loud.

---

"Bella's downstairs," Sofia said, and the tight line of her mouth softened, just a little. "She's been waiting since before the birds were up. Said she wasn't going to let you be late to your own awakening."

Jaiden felt something loosen in his chest. Bella.

He grabbed his coat and the small cloth bundle Sofia had left by the door — clean clothes for after, a ribbon she insisted he'd need, things a mother packed when she couldn't stand in the crowd and watch herself — and he went down.

Bella Rose was leaning against the front gate in the thin grey light, and she was practically vibrating.

"There you are," she said, and her whole face was bright with it — that particular, uncomplicated brightness Jaiden had known since they were children running through the fields behind the house, since before he could remember a time when Bella was not somewhere in his peripheral vision, waiting for him. "Do you have any idea what time it is? Do you have any idea what happens to the last person to arrive at an awakening? They tell everyone. *Everyone.* For the rest of your life, Jaiden Marlock, you would be the boy who was late to his own—"

"I'm coming, I'm coming."

They walked toward the Academy, and the town woke around them. Every road led the same way this morning. Families walked in small, quiet clusters toward the gates, the children in the middle, the parents trying not to look as terrified as the children felt. Jaiden watched them and felt the strange weight of the day settle over everything.

Bella walked half a step ahead of him, chattering about nothing, and he let her fill the silence, because Bella's voice had always had a way of making even this morning feel ordinary.

And then, because he was fifteen, and because the thing moving under his skin would not be quiet, he said it.

"Bella. What if it's wrong?"

She stopped walking.

"What if *what's* wrong?"

"The reading," he said. He didn't know how to say the rest. He didn't know how to say: *what if it sees something in me that shouldn't be there.* "What if I'm not— what if there's something—"

"Jaiden." Bella turned to face him, and for one moment the brightness left her face and was replaced by something older, something steady. "You're going to be *fine*. You've been weird your whole life and I still like you. The altar's going to look at you and see exactly who you are, and whoever that is, it's going to be *fine*."

He wanted to believe her. He almost did.

---

The Academy Altar was nothing like Jaiden had imagined.

He'd pictured stone and candles and solemn priests. What he got was a vast hall full of fifteen-year-olds from every realm, packed shoulder to shoulder, whispering, and at the center of it all a raised dais where a ring of pale runes had been carved into the floor, faintly glowing, faintly humming, like something asleep and dreaming.

Names were being read. One by one, the children went up.

Jaiden watched them step onto the ring, watched the runes flare, watched the light that poured out of the altar wash over each face — and he watched the adults at the edges of the hall murmur and mark things down in their heavy books. This was the measurement. The reading. The moment a person's whole life got sorted into a class and a grade and a future.

His hands were cold.

"Bella Rose."

Bella squeezed his arm once — *it'll be fine* — and walked up onto the dais with her head high, and stepped into the ring of runes.

The hall went quiet.

It happened all at once. The runes flared, brighter than they had for any of the others, and the light that rose out of the altar did not so much wash over Bella as *pour* out of her — a tide of pure gold, holy energy so bright and so vast that the candles guttered and the nearest adults took an involuntary step back. It kept rising. It kept *growing*. For a long, impossible moment the whole hall was lit by the light of a single Malakhim girl, and it was not the light of an E-grade, it was not the light of any Malakhim Jaiden had ever seen, and the whisper that ran through the crowd was not admiration.

It was shock.

"By the vow," someone breathed, near the front. "That's not possible."

Jaiden stood frozen in the crowd and watched the light of his oldest friend fill the hall, and he felt it again — that thing moving under his skin, that second heartbeat, layered and wrong — and for the first time in his life, he was afraid of it.

On the dais, Bella Rose stood in the center of a small sun, and she had no idea what she had just done.

And somewhere in the crowd, an old man in the plain robes of the Academy's Headmaster watched her — and then, slowly, his gaze moved to Jaiden.

---

"Jaiden Marlock."

The name cut through the hall like a cold wind, and every head turned.

It was his turn.
