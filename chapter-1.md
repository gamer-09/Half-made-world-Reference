# Chapter 1

"Jaiden. *Jaiden.*"

The voice came from very far away, and I decided, with the full authority of a fifteen-year-old who had been dreaming about something warm, that I could safely ignore it.

"Jaiden Marlock, if you are not out of that bed in the next five seconds I will come up there and I will *not* be gentle."

Sofia. I knew that particular register of her voice the way I knew the creak of the third stair. It was the register that meant she had already said my name twice and was now deciding whether to escalate. I buried my face in the pillow and did the math in my head. Five seconds. I could get another five seconds out of this bed if I pretended to be asleep.

The bedroom door opened.

"Four."

I sat up so fast my head spun.

"I'm awake," I said. "I'm awake, I'm awake—"

Sofia stood in the doorway with her arms crossed, and even half-asleep, bleary and blinking against the light, I registered that she was not in her usual morning mood. Her grey eyes were doing that thing they did when she was worried and pretending not to be, and her mouth was set in a line that meant there was no arguing with what came next.

"Do you know what day it is?" she asked.

I knew. I'd known for weeks. Every fifteen-year-old in the world knew, because it was the one day nobody got to skip, no matter who their parents were, no matter how far from anywhere they lived. But I also knew Sofia well enough to know that she wanted to say it, so I let her.

"Your awakening," she said, and the word landed in the quiet house like a stone in still water. "You're to be at the Academy altar before the ninth bell, and you are still in bed."

---

The first thing I did every morning, before anything else, was touch the pendant.

It hung on a fine chain around my neck, a small ruby the color of deep water, and I had worn it for as long as I could remember. I didn't remember a time without it. It was just *there*, a small, cool weight against my chest, and touching it first thing was as much a habit as breathing. I'd asked Sofia once, when I was small, why I had to wear it always, why I was never allowed to take it off, not for swimming, not for sleeping, not ever.

*Because it keeps you safe,* she'd said. *And because I gave you my word you'd keep it on. Do you understand?*

I'd nodded, the way you nod at a thing you can't possibly understand but know is important. I'd never taken it off. I didn't take it off now. I just curled my fingers around it through my shirt, felt its cool weight settle against my palm, and let it steady me the way it always did.

The house was small, and it had never felt smaller than it did that morning.

I ate the breakfast Sofia put in front of me without tasting it — bread, and an egg, and a cup of water that I drank too fast. Across the table, Sofia was not eating at all. She was watching me, and there was something in the way she watched me that morning that made the food sit wrong in my stomach.

I was used to the way Sofia watched me. She had watched me my whole life, the way other people's parents watched their children at the edge of deep water — not hovering, exactly, but never quite looking away. I'd asked her once, when I was small, why she always looked at me like that.

*Because you're the only thing in this world that's mine,* she'd said. *And I intend to keep you in one piece.*

I'd laughed then. I didn't laugh now.

"Sofia," I said, and stopped.

She looked up.

"Nothing," I said. "I'm nervous."

It wasn't a lie, exactly. It just wasn't the whole truth. The whole truth was that I'd been nervous for weeks, and it had nothing to do with the altar. The whole truth was that sometimes, when I was alone, when the house was quiet and my mind went still, I felt something *moving* under my skin — something that had no name and no reason to be there, something that felt less like a heartbeat and more like two heartbeats, layered on top of each other, faintly, wrong.

And every time I felt it, my hand went to the pendant. Like it knew. Like it was the only thing keeping whatever was under my skin from spilling out.

I had never told Sofia. I didn't know how to put it into words. And some part of me, the part that had been raised to keep secrets the way other children kept toys, understood without being told that this was a thing you did not say out loud.

---

"Bella's downstairs," Sofia said, and the tight line of her mouth softened, just a little. "She's been waiting since before the birds were up. Said she wasn't going to let you be late to your own awakening."

I felt something loosen in my chest. Bella.

I grabbed my coat and the small cloth bundle Sofia had left by the door — clean clothes for after, a ribbon she insisted I'd need, things a mother packed when she couldn't stand in the crowd and watch herself — and I went down.

Bella Rose was leaning against the front gate in the thin grey light, and she was practically vibrating.

"There you are," she said, and her whole face was bright with it — that particular, uncomplicated brightness I'd known since we were children running through the fields behind the house, since before I could remember a time when Bella was not somewhere in my peripheral vision, waiting for me. "Do you have any idea what time it is? Do you have any idea what happens to the last person to arrive at an awakening? They tell everyone. *Everyone.* For the rest of your life, Jaiden Marlock, you would be the boy who was late to his own—"

"I'm coming, I'm coming."

We walked toward the Academy, and the town woke around us. Every road led the same way this morning. Families walked in small, quiet clusters toward the gates, the children in the middle, the parents trying not to look as terrified as the children felt. I watched them and felt the strange weight of the day settle over everything.

Bella walked half a step ahead of me, chattering about nothing, and I let her fill the silence, because Bella's voice had always had a way of making even this morning feel ordinary.

And then, because I was fifteen, and because the thing moving under my skin would not be quiet, I said it.

"Bella. What if it's wrong?"

She stopped walking.

"What if *what's* wrong?"

"The reading," I said. My hand went to the pendant again, automatic, and I felt its cool weight through my shirt. I didn't know how to say the rest. I didn't know how to say: *what if it sees something in me that shouldn't be there.* "What if I'm not— what if there's something—"

"Jaiden." Bella turned to face me, and for one moment the brightness left her face and was replaced by something older, something steady. "You're going to be *fine*. You've been weird your whole life and I still like you. The altar's going to look at you and see exactly who you are, and whoever that is, it's going to be *fine*."

I wanted to believe her. I almost did.

---

The Academy Altar was nothing like I had imagined.

I'd pictured stone and candles and solemn priests. What I got was a vast hall full of fifteen-year-olds from every realm, packed shoulder to shoulder, whispering, and at the center of it all a raised dais where a ring of pale runes had been carved into the floor, faintly glowing, faintly humming, like something asleep and dreaming.

Names were being read. One by one, the children went up.

I watched them step onto the ring, watched the runes flare, watched the light that poured out of the altar wash over each face — and I watched the adults at the edges of the hall murmur and mark things down in their heavy books. This was the measurement. The reading. The moment a person's whole life got sorted into a class and a grade and a future.

My hands were cold. I kept them around the pendant, and it was cold too.

"Bella Rose."

Bella squeezed my arm once — *it'll be fine* — and walked up onto the dais with her head high, and stepped into the ring of runes.

The hall went quiet.

It happened all at once. The runes flared, brighter than they had for any of the others, and the light that rose out of the altar did not so much wash over Bella as *pour* out of her — a tide of pure gold, holy energy so bright and so vast that the candles guttered and the nearest adults took an involuntary step back. It kept rising. It kept *growing*. For a long, impossible moment the whole hall was lit by the light of a single Malakhim girl, and it was not the light of an E-grade, it was not the light of any Malakhim I had ever seen, and the whisper that ran through the crowd was not admiration.

It was shock.

"By the vow," someone breathed, near the front. "That's not possible."

I stood frozen in the crowd and watched the light of my oldest friend fill the hall, and I felt it again — that thing moving under my skin, that second heartbeat, layered and wrong — and for the first time in my life, I was afraid of it.

My hand closed tight around the pendant.

On the dais, Bella Rose stood in the center of a small sun, and she had no idea what she had just done.

And somewhere in the crowd, an old man in the plain robes of the Academy's Headmaster watched her — and then, slowly, his gaze moved to me.

---

"Jaiden Marlock."

The name cut through the hall like a cold wind, and every head turned.

It was my turn.
