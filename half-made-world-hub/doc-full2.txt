[47] The Sundering Altar
[48] At 15, every person in the world is brought to The Sundering Altar, where their latent connection to Veyn is unlocked for the first time. Before this, no one — human, elf, angel, demon — can consciously access magic. The altar doesn't teach them to use it; it simply opens the channel.
[49] The moment Veyn awakens in them, it manifests visibly in a way that reveals their affinity — the specific path/discipline they are suited to (e.g., combat-magic, healing, elemental control, beast-bonding, etc.). This is not a choice the person makes consciously in the moment; it is revealed to them, based on what's already latent inside them — which is why it works well as "awakening" rather than "selection." The path-choosing comes after once someone knows their affinity, they then choose how to pursue it (which order, mentor, discipline, or role to commit to).

[81] Humans
[82] Unlike other races, humans aren't locked into one Veyn affinity for life. When an elf, angel, or demon is revealed at the altar, that affinity stays fixed forever, but a human's affinity can change or grow over time through training. This means a human could eventually learn more than one affinity, something no other race can do.
[83] The catch is that switching or adding a new affinity is extremely hard. Most humans try and give up, choosing to stick with the one affinity they got at the altar instead. Even if the ones who succeed never become as powerful in any single affinity as a race built specifically for it, they trade raw power for versatility.
[84] Veyn
[85] It is what allows people to use magic in the world. It is drawn from the core of the world and everything around us. Veyn naturally glows a pale silver-blue, like faint moonlight. This is its raw, neutral form before it's shaped into something else, holy energy brightens it into gold, while corrupted Veyn (like a demon's miasma) darkens it into deep purple or black.
[86] Ren
[87] It is what swordsmen use in place for Veyn. It is the inner energy from our body brought out of our body to enhance our strength. Ren glows a deep crimson red since it is drawn from a person's own Vitality rather than an outside source. Where Veyn feels cool and ambient, Ren feels warm and physical, more like a pulse than a light.
[88] Healer
[89] There are only seven healers in the Human Realm. When one dies, a new healer is chosen within the next two years. One person in the Human Realm knows who the next healer will be; this role is called The Seer. The Seer is entrusted with a gem known as The Truthstone which has the power to locate who the new healer is. The Seer is the only one who knows the gem’s location.
[90] Fallen Angel
[91] They have black spotted white wings or black cherry spotted white wings and a broken halo on top of their head.
[92] Once the vow breaks, the angel is cast out of the Angel Realm and rejected by their own race, this is what makes them a Fallen Angel. They keep their wings and whatever power they had, but can never take another Veyn Vow again, so their connection to Veyn stays unstable and unregulated. This makes them strong in bursts but dangerous even to themselves since nothing filters how much Veyn moves through them anymore.
[93] The Angel Veyn Vow
[94] Every angel takes a Veyn Vow when their halo first forms, it binds their access to Veyn to their obedience to the angelic hierarchy. It's not just spoken, it's a magical bond, the halo is the physical seal of it. If the vow holds, an angel can freely use Veyn within their rank's limit, and the halo regulates it, so it doesn't overwhelm them.
[95] The vow is the reason angels can hold as much power as they do, without it their body can't safely filter raw Veyn on its own. Rank affects the vow too, a higher rank means a stronger vow and halo, so they can channel more Veyn without breaking.
[96] An Angel Breaking the vow.
[97] The vow breaks when an angel disobeys someone above their rank or pulls more Veyn than their vow allows to act against an order. Once it breaks the halo can't hold the channel steady and shatters, becoming the broken halo Fallen Angels are known for.
[98] The wings get affected too. The uncontrolled Veyn surging through them at that moment leaves permanent black spotting on the feathers, some end up with plain black spots, others with black cherry spotting depending on how much Veyn surged through when it broke.

[295] Lucent Stone→
[296] A refined form of a Veyn Stone, created by purifying the crystallized Veyn even further until it becomes clear and stable. Unlike a raw Veyn Stone, a Lucent Stone holds a much higher concentration of Veyn in a much smaller size, making it far more potent, but also far harder and more costly to produce. It's used in stronger brews and enchantments where a regular Veyn Stone wouldn't be enough, a single Lucent Stone can be worth several raw Veyn Stones combined.

[348] Cindergrave Wraith Lord→
[349] The ruler of Lethmoor's ruins, formed from the strongest fragment of will, pulled from the fallen kingdom when the city was replicated. It's not a dead body turned undead; it's an unfinished will given shape by the unstable Veyn in Lethmoor's terrain. It hungers for Veyn from anything living that enters the ruins and feeding is what keeps its half-replicated form from falling apart. It rules from the throne room but can move through the ruins to rally the lesser Cindergrave wraiths if intruders get deep enough into the city. Its touch drains Veyn directly from a target to sustain itself, the more it feeds, the stronger and more solid it gets for a while before fading back down. Destroying it means disrupting the Veyn holding it together, not just physical damage, so a Diviner or Exorcist hits it far harder than a regular weapon would.
[350] Lesser Cindergrave wraiths→
[351] Weaker fragments of will formed the same way the Cindergrave Wraith Lord was, pulled from the fallen kingdom when Lethmoor was replicated, just without enough strength or Veyn to hold a fully solid form. They drift through the ruins and only fully mobilize when the Cindergrave Wraith Lord rallies them against intruders who get too deep into the city. Like their lord, they feed on Veyn from the living to stay together, though far less efficiently, a lesser Cindergrave wraith can be worn down and dispersed by regular weapons, they just reform again over time as long as Lethmoor's unstable Veyn remains in the terrain to rebuild them from.

[362] Virastra, the Ascendant Vow (a sword)
[363] Lost somewhere in the ruins scattered across the Hidden Realm, no longer in anyone's possession. Unlike Vireth, its blade is untouched, still holding a clean, steady light along its edge, closer to moonlight than sunlight. Where Vireth feels heavy and restless, Virastra is said to feel almost weightless in the hand, an ease that reflects its uncorrupted nature. Being lost among ruins rather than deliberately sealed, its exact location isn't guarded the way Vireth's is, it's simply forgotten, waiting for someone to stumble upon it rather than seek it out. Extremely effective against demons and halts a demon’s regeneration unless the user releases the effect.
[364] Vireth, the Fallen Grace (a sword)
[365] Sealed within the depths of the Sentient River, held down by two separate seals rather than one, as though whoever bound it there didn't trust a single ward to be enough. The blade itself is dark, almost absorbing the light around it rather than reflecting it, with faint cracks of dim gold light bleeding through along the fuller, like the last remnants of what it used to be before it fell. Even sealed, it's said the water around it never fully settles, the current near it moves like something is still restless beneath the surface. Strangely, the blade doesn't feel like something purely angelic or purely demonic, whatever corruption twisted it seems to have left behind a blade that belongs fully to neither side. Very effective against an angel or a demon, it can stop the regeneration of both races effectively unless the user releases the effect. A user bound weapon.

[367] Graceless Edge→
[368] Cuts through both holy wards and demonic wards equally, treating corrupted and uncorrupted Veyn as the same thing to sever, essentially cutting magic if you have enough Veyn to cut it. Where most weapons are either blessed or unholy and struggle against the opposite type, Vireth doesn't distinguish between the two at all.
[369] Split Grace→
[370] Channels both a holy(angelic) and a corrupted(demonic) aura through the blade at the same time, striking with both auras layered into one attack. For most wielders this is dangerous to hold even briefly, since angelic and demonic energy naturally resist coexisting in the same space, let alone the same blade. As a result of the this it causes a force splitting explosion of the cut area.
[371] Sentient River
[372] The Sentient River is aware, and it doesn't take kindly to being disturbed. The water reacts to intent, calm and clear for those who simply pass by, but violent and resistant towards anyone who tries to take something from it, especially the thing sealed in its depths. Currents shift on their own, pulling and dragging with a will rather than gravity, and it's said the river can sense a person's purpose the moment they touch it. Combined with the Sirens who make their home there, hypnotizing anyone who hears them sing, the river isn't just a body of water guarding Vireth, it's an active participant in keeping it buried, one that punishes anyone who comes looking for the wrong reasons.

[375] Leviathan
[376] A massive sea-serpent by nature, not something born of fire or lava, but forced to adapt after being drawn into Lethmoor's territory by its unstable Veyn. Its scales have hardened and darkened over time, taking on a scorched, obsidian-like sheen to withstand the heat, and what was likely once a creature built for deep water now moves through lava the way its kind would normally move through the sea, coiling and submerging beneath the surface rather than swimming through it. It's unclear whether it was drawn in by the concentrated Veyn the way the Hidden Realm's portal is, trapped after the terrain destabilized around it, or simply never left once it adapted, but either way, it's been down there long enough that Lethmoor's ruins are as much its territory now as the throne room is the Cindergrave Wraith Lord's. It surfaces to guard the approach to the kingdom, and its sheer size and adapted resilience to heat make it one of the first real obstacles anyone has to survive before ever reaching the ruins themselves.
[377] Concentrated Veyn
[378] Matches the color of a Lucent Stone: the same silver-blue as raw Veyn, just far brighter and more vivid, clear rather than faint or hazy.
[379] Saturation Sickness
[380] Caused by staying near or within concentrated Veyn for too long. The body can't regulate the overwhelming amount of ambient Veyn, causing a person's own Veyn to leak out constantly rather than being properly contained. This leaves them unable to perform any real magic, at most just basic spells, along with constant weakness, vomiting, and excessive sleep as the body struggles against the imbalance.
