# Chapter 0 — The Half-Made World

Before you step into this story, know what kind of world it is.

It is a world of **three Realms**, where there was once one. The **Human Realm**, green and crowded and full of ordinary days — its capital **Aldermere**, its people the only ones who can reshape their own affinity, the only ones who can master every skill of a class, and the only ones who will all be dead within a mortal lifetime. The **Angel Realm**, its capital **Aurelion**, a nation of wings and halos the size of a single city, where a vow keeps order and the loss of one pillar shakes the whole. And the **Demon Realm**, its capital **Vorath**, a churning place of horns and miasma where rank is seized by killing the one above you, and where the army is endless because the dying never stops.

Between them, everywhere and nowhere, lies the **Hidden Realm** — a country with no sun, where time runs ten times slow and no one hungers. It is the wound the world never healed from. Remember it. It is why everything else is the way it is.

---

Know the magic, because it is not one magic.

**Veyn** flows out of the world core like blood from a heart — a pale silver-blue that every living thing can learn to shape. That is the common gift, the human gift. But Veyn can be pulled two ways. **Holy Energy** is Veyn purified into gold, the light of angels and saints, and it burns whatever is corrupted. **Corrupted Veyn** is Veyn darkened into miasma, the purple-black breath of demons, and it rots whatever it touches. The two cannot meet without cancelling. They are the same pulse, split by a wound, and they have been at war ever since.

And there is **Ren** — the inner fire of a body's own Vitality, crimson and warm, the energy of swordsmen and archers and the ones who fight with nothing but themselves. It cannot share a body with Veyn. A being must choose, and most choose at fifteen.

Because at fifteen, every child of every race is brought to the **Sundering Altar**, where the seal on their power is broken open and their **class** is revealed — Mage, Alchemist, Beastmaster, Smithing, Enchanter, Diviner, Exorcist, Broker, Saintess, Clergy, Archer, Paladin, Grimtender, Bladesinger, Berserker, Sentinel, Ren Archer, Veilrunner, Shudonkeeper. Nineteen paths. And with it, a **Grade** — G to S, the measure of how much Veyn their body holds, from a mortal's spark to a demigod's storm. Mages are the exception: they carve **Rune Stars** into their own hearts instead, one circle at a time, and a single misstep kills them.

They pay for all of it in **Sigils** — silver, gold, platinum — the same coin in all three Realms, because money, unlike faith, crosses borders freely.

---

Know the people, and know that none of them are simple.

The **angels** keep their power behind a **Vow** — a halo that is a seal, a promise that shatters when broken. The **demons** keep theirs behind nothing at all, and it is this — the vow and the absence of it — that makes them what they are. The **humans** keep no halo and no horns, only short years and raw Veyn, and the terrifying freedom to become anything.

And the **Aelthar** — you must know the Aelthar — are gone.

---

Because once, before the Realms, before the barrier, before the altar and the classes and the coins, there was a people who could *shape* worlds, and a king who feared them, and the rest is the wound that made all of this.

The Aelthar were **realm-makers**. They held the shape of a horizon in their hands. They could have unmade the world at any moment, and so, for a thousand years, they chose not to — and that restraint is the whole of their virtue. But a human king, **Aldric Malgrave**, looked at their power and saw only the risk to his crown. He did not make war on them. He made a *hunt*.

Cornered, the Aelthar did the one thing their lore forbade: they tore open the **Hidden Realm** as a hiding place. The seas rose. Every fire in the world went out for a single heartbeat. And the world, which had been whole, remembered it could be torn.

For that, the **world core** — the will at the heart of everything, which has no face and no body and no anger, only a will — answered twice.

First, it **cursed them**. Every Aelthar, down to the last child, wore a rune collar around the neck, a mark that could not be hidden or removed, that woke the Shudon to hunt them and sealed them where they stood. The world does not forgive being nearly unmade.

And second, it **raised the barrier** — split one world into three, and set at every door a **Shudon**, a golem of the core's own stone, so that no single tearing could ever break the whole again.

The Aelthar did not survive their refuge. The Hidden Realm was not finished — it had not decided what it was — and the creatures that formed in it killed them, nearly all. Only one walked back out: a boy, the last of his kind, who learned to endure a world that was still becoming itself.

His name was **Kael von Ashen**.

---

Know, then, what this story is.

It is the story of a world that was **half-made** — torn open and hastily stitched shut, its seams still showing. It is the story of a curse that outlived the people it was laid on, and a barrier that no one alive remembers being raised. It is the story of a school, **Elyndor Academy**, built at the crossing of three Realms by the last Aelthar, where every child in the world comes to be awakened at fifteen and none of them know what the quiet old Headmaster once did — or what he is the last of.

And it is the story of one such child.

He does not know yet that his blood is a seam where two halves of the world meet. He does not know that the two people who made him were an angel and a demon, that his parents loved across the one divide the world says cannot be crossed, or that he is the first of something that has never existed before.

He only knows the quiet house where he grew up, and the woman who raised him, and a small ruby pendant he has been told never to take off.

That is where we begin. But before we do, look once more at the world — at the three Realms, and the gold and the black, and the altar, and the coins, and the seam in the sky no one can see.

Because all of it — every realm, every class, every curse — was already moving toward a single boy, in a single house, waiting for the day the seal came off.
