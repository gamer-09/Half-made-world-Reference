# Chapter 0 — "The Last Door" (a writing suggestion)

> A draft recommendation for opening the *Half-Made World* novel, grounded in the repo's
> established lore. This is a *suggestion*, not a finished chapter — use it as a scaffold, then
> make it your own voice.

---

## What this chapter must do (from the lore)

Chapter 0 is the **origin sin** of the whole world. It has to plant, quietly and without a single
info-dump, the four facts that every later tragedy hangs from:

1. The **former Human king** (I've named him **Aldric Malgrave**) hunted the **Aelthar** — the
   ancient realm-maker race — to extinction.
2. His chase forced the survivors to **tear open the Hidden Realm** as a refuge — an act so violent
   it **nearly unmade the world**.
3. That near-destruction woke the **world core's will**, which raised the **world barrier** and
   carved the **Shudon** to stop the collapse.
4. **One Aelthar survived** — the boy who would become **Kael von Ashen**, Headmaster of Elyndor
   Academy, and the sole keeper of his race's knowledge.

Chapter 1 will open centuries later with Jaiden. So Chapter 0's job is **not** to explain any of
this — it's to make the reader *feel* the fear and the loss, so that when "Hidden Realm,"
"Headmaster," and "Aelthar" reappear later, they already carry weight.

## Modern prologue technique (what I'm applying)

From how current fantasy handles openings (*The Name of the Wind*, *A Game of Thrones*, *The Lies
of Locke Lamora*, *Fourth Wing*):

1. **One POV, one emotion.** Don't pan a camera over a kingdom. Live inside *one* person.
2. **Show, don't tell.** The reader learns the king is a monster because of what he *does*, not
   because a narrator says so.
3. **No info-dumps.** No paragraphs explaining realm-makers or Veyn. Let a child's fear carry it.
4. **Short.** A prologue should be roughly half a normal chapter. This one can be 800–1,500 words.
5. **End on a pull.** The last line should make it impossible not to turn the page.

---

## Suggested structure

### 1. Cold open — inside the fear (not the history)

Open on the **Aelthar fleeing**, not on the king. A small, concrete moment — a parent's hand on a
child's shoulder, the sound of the hunt behind them. The reader doesn't yet know *what* they are or
*why* they run. That's the hook: **something is chasing them, and it is close.**

*First-line candidates (aim for tension + a question):*
- *"The king's men did not shout when they came. That was the worst of it."*
- *"They had been running so long the child no longer remembered the sound of her own name."*
- *"The last of the Aelthar counted the torches in the dark and knew they were one short."*

### 2. The pursuer — Aldric, shown, never explained

Introduce Malgrave through a **single, humanizing-then-horrifying beat**. He is calm. He believes,
genuinely, that he is saving the Human Realm from a threat. He says something almost kind to a
soldier, then gives the order that ends a life. The horror is in the *contrast* — a reasonable man
doing an unreasonable thing.

*Keep him off-stage or half-seen.* A prologue works better if the reader senses his weight through
the hunted than through his own mouth. (The `rules → Human Realm`, `kills → Aelthar`,
`hunted → Kael von Ashen` links are the spine of this scene.)

### 3. The tearing — the Hidden Realm is born

The emotional climax: cornered, the survivors do the thing the lore describes — **they tear open
the Hidden Realm.** Write it as an act of *desperation*, not triumph. The act is violent; it
*should* feel like it nearly breaks the world (because it did). A seam of light. The world
shudders. And they step through, believing they are safe.

*This is where "show, don't tell" does the heaviest lifting:* the reader should feel the world
nearly come apart in their hands, without a single line of exposition about the world core.

### 4. The will that answered (implied, not explained)

After the tear, a beat of **something older than the king** noticing. No lecture. One image: the
ground settling, a light from deep below, the faint grind of stone — the **world core's will**
stirring, and the **Shudon** being carved in the dark. Keep it to two or three sentences. The
reader shouldn't understand it yet; they should only *feel* that the hunters have awoken something
worse than themselves.

### 5. The survivor — the emotional landing

End on the **one who got away.** Not a victory speech. A child (or a young scholar) alone in the
new realm, watching the first of the realm's creatures form in the dark, understanding with a
child's clarity that the refuge has become a tomb — and that they are the last.

*Final-line candidates (must leave a question):*
- *"She was the last of her people, and she was not yet ten."*
- *"In the dark of the realm they had built to be safe, the creatures were already forming."*
- *"He did not know it yet, but he would be the only one to walk back out."*

---

## The one rule to hold

**Do not mention Veyn, the world core, the barrier, or the Shudon by name.** Not once. This
chapter is a *feeling*, not a wiki. Those names belong to Chapter 1 and beyond, where they will
land with the weight Chapter 0 earned for them.

If a reader can close Chapter 0 and say *"something terrible happened, a whole people is gone, and
one child survived"* — without being able to quote a single piece of world-building — you've done
it right.
